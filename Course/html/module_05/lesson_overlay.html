Text Overlay: Three Techniques, One Goal

All three techniques aim to show a viewer a different value than the one originally written.

📌 1. Annotation-Based Overlay

A /FreeText annotation is placed over existing text with an opaque white background, hiding the original and displaying a replacement value.

% Annotation dictionary — object 7: 7 0 obj << /Type /Annot /Subtype /FreeText /Rect [68 648 320 668] ← positioned over original date /Contents (Date: Wednesday, 19 March 2025) ← replacement value /MK << /BG [1 1 1] >> ← WHITE background — covers original /CreationDate (D:20250320091500+00'00') ← added 5 days after creation >> endobj
ℹ️
What to Look For

/MK /BG [1 1 1] = opaque white background; /Rect overlaps a known text region; /CreationDate is later than the document /CreationDate; /Contents differs from the underlying text.

⬜ 2. Content Stream White-Box

A white-filled rectangle is drawn directly in the page content stream, covering the original text. Replacement text is then drawn at the same position.

% Page content stream excerpt — decoded: BT /F1 10 Tf 72 636 Td (Total: $8,200) Tj ET ← ORIGINAL (buried) q 1 1 1 rg ← set fill colour to WHITE 68 630 120 16 re ← define rectangle f ← FILL (covers "$8,200") Q BT /F1 10 Tf 72 636 Td (Total: $12,450) Tj ET ← REPLACEMENT
⚠️
Detection Note

No annotation panel, no annotation list — this technique is entirely within the page content stream. Search decoded streams for 1 1 1 rg followed by re and f.

🔤 3. Text-Over-Text

Replacement glyphs are drawn at exactly the same coordinates as the original glyphs. No masking rectangle, no annotation.

% Original text: BT /F1 10 Tf 155 656 Td (Sat) Tj ET % Replacement at SAME coordinates: BT /F1 10 Tf 155 656 Td (Wed) Tj ET
🚨
Hardest to Detect

Text-over-text has no annotation in /Annots, no white rectangle, no visual artifact in normal view. Detection requires decoding the content stream and finding two BT...ET blocks with overlapping character positions. This is used by Adobe Acrobat Reader Desktop's Fill & Sign.
